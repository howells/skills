# Slop names

The stock names language models reach for when they invent a person, place or
company for an example, sample data, a story or a demo. One such name proves nothing.
A cast of them, or one that does not fit the setting, is a tell that nobody chose the
name.

The lists move with each model release and family; treat them as a snapshot from
late 2026. The measured studies used creative-writing prompts ("write a story", "a
fictional expert"); the habit carries into business examples, test data and demos,
but only anecdotes say so. Never explain the cause in an edit note.

## First names and full names

Measured in controlled samples:

- **Elara.** The best-supported name, in every model family. Thousands of times more
  frequent than in human text, and in about one story in eight. "Elara Voss" is the
  GPT default for a fictional expert.
- **Elias, Mara.** In a sixth and a seventh of generated stories respectively.
- **Marcus, Marcus Chen.** Claude above all: "Marcus Chen" for a novel's software
  developer 100 times out of 100, "Marcus" for nearly a quarter of male names drawn.
  Common in other families too.
- **Elena Vasquez.** Claude's fictional expert, fading in newer checkpoints. "Elena
  Rodriguez" was the older Claude default.
- **Amara Okafor (or Okonkwo).** The third of a Claude trio with Elena Vasquez and
  Marcus Chen, mostly on the Opus line, dead by late 2025.
- **Aris Thorne, Lena Petrova.** Gemini, singly and as a pair.
- **Eleanor, Thomas, Kira Voss.** Claude role prompts: Eleanor for a protagonist's
  wife or a woman in York, Thomas for a man in York, Kira (Voss, Vance, Vex) for a
  spaceship pilot.
- **Sarah Chen.** Measured only low (0 to 13% of fictional-expert prompts by
  checkpoint); its reputation as Claude's go-to expert rests on anecdote.
- **Clara, Lila, Maya, Mira, Elian.** Lower but frequent in stories. "Lila" is the
  measured one, not "Lily".

Reported, no count behind it:

- **Priya, Priya Shah, Priya Sharma.** The strongest anecdote here: flag it in Claude
  output on sight. Invented colleagues, sales reps, customers and experts called
  Priya, most often Priya Shah; reported as the name displacing Elena in newer Claude
  output. If asked why, say it is reported, not measured.
- **Sarah Martinez, Teresa Chen.** In a Claude-written novel.
- **Kai, Lyra, Lena.** Reported as consistent Claude or chatbot choices.
- **Kael, Kaelen.** Next to Elara as a fixated fantasy name; called ChatGPT's top male
  name by one reader.
- **Lily.** One unretrieved source.
- **Dr. Elara Voss and variants:** Elara Vex, Elena Voss, Elias Vance, Dr. Elara
  Finch, Dr. Elara Solis. Dozens of Amazon books are credited to "Elara Voss".
- **A published blocklist** also ranks Eira, Thorne, Hawk, Arin and Emilia alongside
  the names above.

Searched for, nothing ties them to a model: Patel, Shah and Nakamura as surnames;
Willowbrook, Millbrook and Oakhaven as places; Acme and similar companies. Flag them,
if at all, as generic stock choices, not slop names.

## Surnames

Surnames hold while first names drift, so a swapped first name ("Marcus Williams",
"Victoria Chen") leaves the tell in place.

- **Chen.** Claude: Marcus, Michael, David, Sarah, Mara and Victoria Chen, including
  on characters in 1920s France.
- **Vasquez, older Rodriguez.** Claude.
- **Okafor, Okonkwo.** Claude's Opus line.
- **Voss, Vance, Vex.** Across families, whenever a surname is required.
- **Thorne, Petrova.** Gemini; "Dr Thorne" is everywhere in generated sci-fi.
- **Hargreaves, Hartley, Ashworth, Whitby.** Claude asked for a person in York. Asking
  for a region returns a small stock pool for it, not the region's range of names.
- **Sharma.** Priya, Anya and Anika Sharma on fake pages.

## Places, settings and roles

- **Eldoria, then Aethelgard.** ChatGPT fantasy; hundreds of AI-generated books use
  them.
- **Whispering Woods, Eldora.** Reported secondhand.
- **Lighthouse, village, attic.** The measured settings; the lighthouse is in about
  half of generated stories.
- **Keeper, baker, mayor, clockmaker, fisherman, librarian.** The measured roles. The
  pattern is a name, a trade and a place arriving as a set (Elias, lighthouse
  keeper), which half of generated stories hold.

## Edits

- **Name the role or drop the name.** If the draft invents a person only to carry an
  example, "a sales rep" does the job.
- **Take names from the setting**: the region, decade, age, role and community the
  example is about, sourced from something real (a census or registry surname table,
  a staff directory, the user's contacts, names already in the draft).
- **Vary across a set** in origin, length, first letter and age. The names above lean
  on E, L and M initials and -a endings.
- **Do not swap stock for stock.** Sarah Chen to Priya Shah, or Elara to Lyra, keeps
  the tell. Any list a model produces for you, including replacement tokens such as
  Madelyne, Nadia, Freya and Isolde, is the next stock list.
- **Check fit first.** A Chen in 1920s France, a Vasquez in York, a Hargreaves at a
  Lagos fintech: fix the match before the variety.
- **Real names stay.** Quoted material, named sources and real people keep their
  names.

## What not to flag

- One common name in a document. Chen is among the most common surnames in the world;
  Marcus, Sarah and Maya are ordinary names.
- A name the writer gave or can explain, or one that plainly fits a family, client or
  place the text already describes.
- A name in text from before November 2022.

The tell is the cast taken together (Elena Vasquez, Marcus Chen and Amara Okafor in
one scenario), a trade-place-name set, or a stock name in a setting that does not fit.
