# Slop names

The stock names a language model reaches for when it invents a person, place or
company for an example, sample data, a story or a demo. One such name is ordinary. A
cast of them, or one that does not fit the setting, shows that nobody chose the name.
The lists drift; treat them as a snapshot.

## The names

- **First names:** Elara, Elias, Elian, Elena, Eleanor, Mara, Marcus, Maya, Mira,
  Clara, Lila, Lena, Lyra, Kai, Kira, Kael, Kaelen, Amara, Aris, Priya, Sarah, Thomas.
- **Surnames:** Chen, Voss, Vance, Vex, Vasquez, Rodriguez, Okafor, Okonkwo, Thorne,
  Petrova, Shah, Sharma, Martinez, Finch, Solis; for an English setting Hargreaves,
  Hartley, Ashworth, Whitby.
- **Full names:** Elara Voss, Marcus Chen, Sarah Chen, Elena Vasquez, Amara Okafor,
  Priya Shah, Priya Sharma, Aris Thorne, Lena Petrova. Surnames hold while first
  names drift, so "Marcus Williams" or "Victoria Chen" leaves the tell in place.
- **Places:** Eldoria, Aethelgard, Eldora, Whispering Woods.
- **Sets:** a name, a trade and a place arriving together (Elias, lighthouse keeper):
  keeper, baker, mayor, clockmaker, fisherman or librarian, in a lighthouse, village
  or attic.

## Edits

- **Name the role or drop the name.** If the draft invents a person only to carry an
  example, "a sales rep" does the job.
- **Take names from the setting**: the region, decade, age, role and community the
  example is about, sourced from something real (a census or registry surname table,
  a staff directory, the user's contacts, names already in the draft).
- **Vary across a set** in origin, length, first letter and age. The names above lean
  on E, L and M initials and -a endings.
- **Do not swap stock for stock.** Sarah Chen to Priya Shah, or Elara to Lyra, keeps
  the tell. Any list a model produces for you is the next stock list.
- **Check fit first.** A Chen in 1920s France, a Vasquez in York, a Hargreaves at a
  Lagos fintech: fix the match before the variety.
- **Real names stay.** Quoted material, named sources and real people keep their
  names.

## What not to flag

- One common name in a document. Chen is among the most common surnames in the world;
  Marcus, Sarah and Maya are ordinary names.
- A name the writer gave or can explain, or one that plainly fits a family, client or
  place the text already describes.
